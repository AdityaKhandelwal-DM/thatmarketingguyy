Google ads for restaurants can fill tables, but only if you measure the right thing. A click is not a booking. A phone call, a "get directions" tap or a reservation is. I'm Aditya, a solo Google Ads and Meta Ads consultant working remotely from India, and restaurants are one of the categories I've run the most.

This post covers how I set up Google ads for a restaurant, what to track, and how to tell whether your agency is filling tables or just buying clicks.

## Do Google Ads work for restaurants?

Yes, when someone is searching for somewhere to eat right now. Google puts you in front of people who typed "dinner near me" or "birthday lunch in [your area]", which is a very different audience from someone scrolling Instagram.

They work less well if your whole plan is brand awareness, or if your listing is thin. If your Google Business Profile has no photos, wrong hours or no menu, ads send people to a weak page. Fix that first.

Restaurants also vary a lot. A neighbourhood café, a fine-dining room and a cloud kitchen need different setups. That's why I start with one outlet, one goal and a short list of searches.

## What kind of Google ads should a restaurant run?

For a local food business, I usually start with two things:

1. **Search ads on local intent.** Keywords like "restaurant near me", cuisine plus area, or "private dining" plus city. These catch people who are ready to choose.
2. **Google Business Profile and map-led ads.** These put your outlet on Maps, with the call button and directions one tap away. This matters for restaurant Google Maps ads, because most diners decide on the phone, close to the venue.

If you have several outlets, I run them separately, so you can see which location gets the calls.

## What I count as a result

I don't report clicks as the headline. For a restaurant I track:

- Phone calls from the ad
- "Get directions" taps
- Online reservation or order-form submissions
- Cost for each of those

On my [results page](/results), a restaurant's Google Business Profile ads got 3,857 actions at ₹2.68 (about $0.03) each, from ₹10K of spend. Those prices are Indian prices. In the US, UK, UAE, Australia or Singapore, clicks and calls cost far more, so please don't expect those numbers in your market. What carries over is the method: general local keywords, run per outlet, judged on actions.

## How do I make sure ads fill tables, not just get clicks?

Three habits make the difference:

- **Match the ad to the moment.** Search for "brunch near me" on Saturday morning needs a brunch ad, not your generic homepage.
- **Schedule around service.** Showing ads at 3 a.m. is wasted money. I set ad hours around when people decide where to eat.
- **Answer the phone.** I've seen leads go cold because nobody called back. On my results page, one nightlife venue's leads were never being called back, and I had to restructure the campaign around conversations. The ad can't fix a missed call.

Also add negative keywords early, such as jobs, recipes and delivery apps if you don't deliver. Otherwise you pay for people who never wanted a table.

## Should I use Google or Meta for my restaurant?

Often both, but in an order. Google catches people who are already hungry and searching. Meta (Facebook and Instagram) creates interest, which suits offers, events and party bookings. For example, my results page lists a restaurant party-bookings campaign on Meta that produced 301 leads at ₹77 (about $0.92) each.

If budget is tight, I'd start with Google for walk-in and reservation intent, then add Meta for events. My post on [Facebook ads vs Google ads for a local business](/blog/facebook-ads-vs-google-ads-for-local-business) explains how I'd choose.

## A hypothetical example

This is an illustration, not a client result. Say a restaurant spends $20 a day and gets 40 calls and direction taps in a month. If the spend is about $600, that's roughly $15 per action. If half of those people book a table, each booking costs around $30. Whether that is good depends on how much a table spends. Work that number out before you hire anyone.

## The short version

Target hungry people nearby, track calls and directions, run each outlet separately and answer your phone. Ignore click counts on their own.
