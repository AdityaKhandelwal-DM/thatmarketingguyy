Google Ads for small business can look like a game only big budgets win, so it helps to read how three small UK brands actually grew with it. One started with a shop in Brighton, one was born in Manchester, and one began on a market stall. Each published its own numbers on Google's success-story pages, and I've added what I think they did right and what you can copy.

These figures are self-reported, written up by Google with data the businesses supplied. Treat them as a method to learn from, not a promise for your account.

## The three brands at a glance

- **Lucy & Yak** (clothing, first store in Brighton in 2019): Shopping and Search, then Performance Max. 233% revenue growth year on year. [Source](https://business.google.com/uk/resources/success-stories/lucy-and-yak/).
- **Specscart** (online eyewear, born in Manchester): Search first, then Performance Max. 3.8x ROAS and £4 million revenue through Google Ads. [Source](https://business.google.com/uk/resources/success-stories/specscart/).
- **Bird & Blend Tea Co.** (started at local food markets in 2012, Brighton): Performance Max plus more Search. 439% ROAS in 2023. [Source](https://business.google.com/uk/resources/success-stories/bird-and-blend/).

## Lucy & Yak: 233% revenue growth in a year

### The challenge

Lucy & Yak sold clothing, with a first physical store in Brighton in 2019. Online, it wanted growth from Google without simply paying to be found by people who would have bought anyway.

### What they did

According to Google's write-up, the brand ran Shopping and Search from 2018 and moved to Performance Max by summer 2022. It also widened its match types and set separate goals for paid and organic search, so the two weren't fighting over the same credit.

### The results

Google reports a 233% increase in revenue year on year and a 209% increase in traffic. 54% of orders through paid Search came from new customers. Moving to Performance Max drove a 32% uplift in revenue.

## Specscart: 3.8x ROAS and £4 million through Google Ads

### The challenge

Specscart is an online eyewear seller that grew out of Manchester. Its problem was a common one: how to scale spend without letting efficiency slide.

### What they did

In 2021 it moved to high-intent Search. It then split its budget into brand campaigns and sales campaigns, and added Performance Max once it was already capturing demand. It also adjusted daily spend to match when customers actually buy.

### The results

The published figures are 10X brand growth, 3.8x ROAS, 122,000 orders, and Google Ads accounting for 76% of total sales by 2024, with £4 million of revenue through Google Ads. Brand campaigns returned 8.5x ROAS and sales campaigns 3.8x. The daily spend adjustments gave a 22% efficiency gain.

## Bird & Blend Tea Co.: 439% ROAS in 2023

### The challenge

Bird & Blend began at local food markets in 2012 and is based in Brighton. Selling loose-leaf tea online meant finding new customers, not just serving people who already knew the name.

### What they did

Per the case study, it paired Performance Max with increased investment in Search. The data was provided by the business.

### The results

ROAS reached 439% in 2023. New-customer sales grew 320% since it scaled Google Ads in 2020, brand growth was 200% in two and a half years, and Google Ads now drives 20–25% of online revenue.

## Why it worked

Putting the three stories side by side, I see the same four things, and none of them is a secret setting.

1. **They started with Search.** Specscart and Lucy & Yak ran Search and Shopping for years before Performance Max. Search showed what people were already looking for.
2. **They split brand from non-brand.** Specscart's brand campaigns returned 8.5x and its sales campaigns 3.8x. Blend the two in one report and the brand campaign hides how the rest is doing.
3. **They cared about new customers.** Lucy & Yak reports the share of paid Search orders from new customers. Bird & Blend measures new-customer sales. Counting repeat buyers as "ads working" flatters the numbers.
4. **They treated budget as something to adjust.** Specscart tuned daily spend and got a 22% efficiency gain.

One caution I'd add: all three are online shops with a basket and a clear revenue figure. A local service business will measure calls or store visits instead, and the numbers look very different.

## How to apply this to your business

1. **Start with Search on the terms that mean "I want to buy".** Leave broad reach for later.
2. **Add a small brand campaign** on your own name and keep it in a separate report.
3. **Write down your margin.** Break-even ROAS is 1 divided by your profit margin. A 40% margin breaks even at 2.5x (a hypothetical example).
4. **Track the real action**: a purchase, a call, a booking, not a page view.
5. **Report new versus returning customers** if your platform can.
6. **Add Performance Max only once Search tracking is clean**, and check it monthly.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is a good ROAS for Google Ads?

It depends on your margin. Divide 1 by your profit margin to find break-even ROAS, so a 25% margin needs 4x just to break even. Specscart's 3.8x and Bird & Blend's 439% are their numbers, not a benchmark for you.

### Should a small business start with Performance Max or Search?

All three brands here started with Search and Shopping and brought in Performance Max later. Search shows you which queries convert, and Performance Max works better with that data behind it.

### Are Google's success stories reliable?

They're self-reported and written to promote Google, so I read them for the method. The figures come from the businesses, and none of them tell you what it will do in your market.
