If you run a clinic or dental practice, medical practice Google Ads can fill your diary with people who are already searching for a treatment. It can also burn a month of budget on clicks that never book. I'm Aditya, a solo Google Ads and Meta Ads consultant, and clinics are among the accounts I've run.

This post covers what a new patient really costs, how to judge a number someone quotes you, and what I'd check before spending a dollar.

## Are Google Ads worth it for a clinic?

Yes, for most clinics, because Google catches people at the moment they want an appointment. Someone typing "dentist near me" or a treatment name is not browsing. They have a problem and want it fixed.

That intent is why I start clinics on Google rather than Meta when budget is tight. It only works if three things are in place:

1. **Tracking:** calls, form fills and direction requests are counted, so you know which clicks became enquiries.
2. **A page that books:** a clear phone number and a short form, not a long brochure.
3. **Someone answering:** an enquiry that sits unanswered for a day is paid-for and wasted.

If you can't cover those, fix them before you raise the budget.

## What's a good cost per patient enquiry?

There is no universal number, and I'm wary of anyone who quotes one. Cost per enquiry depends on your country, your treatment, your competitors and how good your page is. A cosmetic procedure in Dubai will not cost what a check-up in a small UK town costs.

What I can show you is real accounts. A physio clinic's YouTube campaign, optimised for conversions instead of views, got 921 conversions at ₹4.47 (about $0.05) each, an 18.6× return on ₹4.1K spend. A restaurant's Google Business Profile search ads got 3,857 actions at ₹2.68 (about $0.03). Those are Indian prices, and clicks in the US, UK, UAE, Australia or Singapore cost far more. Copy the method, not the numbers: count the right actions and check cost per action regularly.

A better test than "is it cheap" is this simple sum. Here is a **hypothetical example**, not a client result. If a new patient is worth $400 to you in the first year and you convert one in four enquiries, you can afford up to $100 per enquiry before you lose money. Your numbers will differ, so work yours out first.

## What does a new patient really cost, then?

A new patient costs your cost per enquiry divided by the share of enquiries that become booked patients. The second figure is the one most owners never measure.

Say your ads bring 40 enquiries a month and 8 book. That is 20%, so each patient costs five times your cost per enquiry. If reception answers faster and that goes to 12 bookings, your cost per patient falls by a third without touching the ads. I've seen this on my own results page: a nightlife venue's form leads at ₹2,264 each were never being called back. The ad wasn't the problem.

## Medical practice Google Ads: what I'd do in the first 90 days

- **Week 1:** set up conversion tracking for calls, forms and directions, and a Google Business Profile that matches your real address and hours.
- **Weeks 2 to 4:** run Search campaigns on a short list of treatment keywords, and build a negative keyword list from the actual search terms.
- **Month 2 onward:** cut what doesn't produce enquiries, move budget to what does, and review the whole thing with you in writing.

The negative list matters more than people think. On my results page I list an education campaign where ₹4.79L (about $5.7K) was spent for 157 conversions, because I used broad keywords with no negatives. I also list a Smart campaign that took 16,326 clicks for one conversion, because Smart campaigns hide the search terms. I now check search terms regularly on every account.

## Google Business Profile for doctors

A Google Business Profile is the free listing that shows your clinic on Maps and in the local results, and for a clinic it is worth getting right before or alongside ads. Keep your name, address, phone number and hours identical everywhere, and choose your categories carefully.

One skin clinic client in Jaipur combined Meta lead ads with a Google Map Pack push and saw 78% more walk-ins and 5.0× ROAS over 90 days on ₹8L+ (about $9.6K) of spend. That was one Jaipur clinic using both channels, not a promise for your city.

One caution: healthcare ads and listings have their own rules, and they differ by country and treatment. Check Google's current policy for your location before launching anything, and have your ads reviewed against it.
