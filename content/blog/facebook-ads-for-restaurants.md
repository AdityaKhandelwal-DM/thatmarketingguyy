Facebook ads for restaurants work when you stop asking for "reach" and start asking for one specific thing: a booking, a message or a visit this week. Most restaurant owners I talk to have already tried boosting a post and got likes from people who live nowhere near the venue. This playbook shows how I set up Facebook ads for a restaurant, step by step, so you can run them yourself or judge whoever you hire.

I'm Aditya, a solo Meta Ads and Google Ads consultant working remotely from India. Restaurants and lounges are one of the categories I've run most, and I'll use real campaigns from my [results page](/results) as examples. Those costs are Indian costs, so I'll give a dollar figure alongside the rupees, but please expect to pay more per lead in the US, UK, UAE, Australia or Singapore.

## Do Facebook ads work for restaurants?

Yes, when the ad asks for one clear action from people close to the venue: book a table, message you or come in this weekend. They work badly when the goal is vague, like "get more followers", or when the offer is the same all year. Facebook and Instagram ads run through Meta, so one campaign can reach both.

They also don't replace being easy to find on Google. If someone is already hungry and searching, my post on [Google Ads for restaurants](/blog/google-ads-for-restaurants) covers that side.

## What's different about restaurants

A restaurant isn't an online shop, and three things change how I build the campaign.

- **Demand follows the clock and the calendar.** Lunch, dinner, weekends, festivals, set menus. An ad needs a reason to come this week, and a schedule that matches when people decide where to eat.
- **It is hyper-local.** Most diners will travel only a few kilometres or miles. The radius and the creative ("two minutes from the station") matter more than clever interest targeting.
- **Different goals need different campaigns.** Walk-ins and awareness, table reservations, party and group bookings, and delivery orders are four separate jobs. Mixing them in one campaign is the most common reason restaurant ads feel like they don't work.

## How to set up Facebook ads for a restaurant, step by step

### 1. Pick one goal per campaign

Decide what a good week looks like. If it's empty tables on weeknights, that is a reservations campaign. If a new outlet just opened, it is local awareness. If you sell party hall slots, it is enquiries. One goal, one campaign.

Use Ads Manager for this, not the "Boost post" button. Boosting is fine for a quick reminder, but it gives you fewer goals and less control over who sees the ad.

### 2. Set a tight local area

Drop a pin on the restaurant and set a radius that matches how far your guests really travel. For a neighbourhood café that may be a few kilometres. For a destination venue it may be a whole city. Run each outlet as its own campaign, so you can see which location is working.

Don't pile on interests. "People who like food" is most of the internet. Location, age range and a good creative do most of the targeting for you.

### 3. Write an offer with a date on it

"Come and dine with us" gives nobody a reason to act today. These do:

- A set menu or tasting evening on a named date
- A weeknight offer for the slow nights only
- A party package with a price and a minimum group size
- A festival or season menu that ends

### 4. Use your own footage

Phone video from the kitchen, the pass and the dining room beats polished stock every time. People can tell. Show the actual dish, the actual room and your actual staff. In one campaign I ran for a banquet and restaurant, I routed creator content into conversation ads and tracked a cost per enquiry for the first time. Before that, nobody knew what the creator spend was buying.

![Dishes shot on the restaurant's own phone footage tend to outperform stock photos in local ads.](fb_partyfood)

### 5. Choose how people respond: form or message

- **Instant form** (a lead ad): the guest taps and fills a short form inside Facebook. Good for reservations and party enquiries. Ask only for name, phone number, date and group size. Every extra field loses people. Meta explains the format in its [Business Help Centre](https://www.facebook.com/business/help).
- **Message ad**: the guest opens a WhatsApp or Messenger chat. Good for banquets and private dining, where people want to ask questions first.
- **Link to your booking page**: fine if the booking page loads fast on a phone. If it doesn't, an instant form will do better.

### 6. Track the real result

Count reservations, enquiries and chats, not likes. If your booking system can't tell you where a booking came from, ask guests "how did you hear about us?" when they book, and keep a simple tally. A weekly tally from the front desk is better than a dashboard nobody checks.

### 7. Set a budget and a schedule

Work backwards. Say a table of four spends about $80 (a hypothetical figure, use your own average bill). If you want 20 extra covered tables a month and expect roughly one in four enquiries to turn up, you need about 80 enquiries. Divide your monthly budget by 80 and you know the most you can pay per enquiry. If the maths doesn't work, change the offer before you raise the budget.

Show ads in the hours before people decide, not at 3 a.m. For dinner, that is mostly the afternoon and early evening.

## Ad examples you can adapt

These are examples to adapt, not real client ads.

- **Reservations:** "Friday tasting menu, six courses, $45. Twelve tables only. Reserve yours below." Button: Book now (instant form).
- **Party bookings:** "Birthday dinner for 10 or more? One set menu, one price, a table by the window. Tell us the date and we'll confirm today." Button: Get quote.
- **Local awareness for a new place:** "We just opened two streets from you. Come for lunch this week." Short video of the room and one dish.

## What results to expect

Judge the campaign on cost per booking or enquiry, not on reach. These are my own campaigns, all in India, and they're here to show what each goal looks like, not what you will pay in your market:

- **Rooftop lounge, reservations:** instant-form reservation ads replaced generic traffic pushes. 382 leads at ₹38 (about $0.45) each, 1.82% click-through rate, on ₹15K (about $180) of spend.
- **Restaurant, party bookings:** instant-form party enquiry ads built per outlet, not per brand. 301 leads at ₹77 (about $0.92), 2.03% CTR, ₹23K (about $275).
- **Banquet and restaurant, chats:** creator content into conversation ads. ₹9.92 (about $0.12) per chat, 4.5L (450,000) reach, ₹14K (about $168).
- **New café, local awareness:** reach ads held at a frequency of 1.0, so each person saw the ad once. CPM of ₹1.61 and 2.1L (210,000) reach on just ₹336 (about $4).

The café is the odd one out, and I want to be honest about that: it is a reach campaign. It told the neighbourhood the place existed, but I'm not claiming it filled tables on its own. Treat reach as the cheap first step and reservations or chats as the proof.

## Mistakes I see most

- Running one campaign for walk-ins, bookings and delivery, so none of them can be judged
- Using the same offer all year
- Targeting the whole city when guests come from a few kilometres away
- Sending ad clicks to a slow booking page or a PDF menu
- Leaving enquiries unanswered. A lead that waits a day is usually gone, and no ad can fix that
- Judging the ad by likes and comments

## Frequently asked questions

### How much should a restaurant spend on Facebook ads?

Work back from your average bill and the covers you want. Hypothetically, if a booking is worth $80 and you can afford to pay $10 per enquiry, you know your limit before you spend anything. Start small, test for a few weeks and scale only what produces bookings.

### Should a restaurant boost posts or use Ads Manager?

For reservations, enquiries and chats, use Ads Manager, because it lets you choose the goal and the audience. Boosting is fine for a quick reminder to people who already follow you, but it gives you less control.

### What is the best Facebook ad for a restaurant?

A specific dish or offer, shot on your own phone, with one clear button: book now or message us. Generic "come and eat with us" ads tend to be the weakest.
