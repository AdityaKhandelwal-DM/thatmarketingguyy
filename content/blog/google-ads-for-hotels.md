Google Ads for hotels can win back the guests you currently pay a booking site commission to reach, but only if the campaign is built around direct bookings, calls and direction requests rather than clicks. Most independent hotel owners I speak to already know the pain: a guest searches your name, lands on a travel site, books there, and you pay commission on someone who was already looking for you.

This guide shows how I set up Google Ads for a hotel, step by step, so you can run it yourself or judge whoever you hire. I'm Aditya, a solo Google Ads and Meta Ads consultant working remotely from India with small businesses overseas. One thing up front: I've run a hotel campaign, not a hotel chain's whole search programme, and my costs are Indian costs. Expect the method to carry over, not the prices.

## Do Google Ads work for hotels?

Yes, for two kinds of search. The first is people typing your hotel's name, where a small campaign keeps the booking with you instead of a travel site. The second is people searching close to where you are, such as "hotel near [landmark]" or "guesthouse in [neighbourhood]", where they're ready to choose.

They work less well if your plan is to outbid big travel sites on broad searches like "hotels in [city]". Those sites have far bigger budgets and you'll usually lose that fight. Pick the searches where being the hotel itself is an advantage.

## What's different about hotels

A hotel isn't a shop or a clinic, and five things change how I build the account.

- **You're competing with your own distributors.** Travel sites often bid on your hotel's name. A brand campaign is cheap protection that most small hotels never switch on.
- **The result isn't always a form.** Guests book on your booking engine, phone the front desk, or tap "get directions" and just turn up. All three are real results and all three need tracking.
- **Demand is seasonal.** Peak weeks, school holidays, festivals and quiet months need different budgets, so one flat daily figure wastes money in the slow months.
- **Your booking engine decides what you can measure.** If it can't pass a booking back to Google, you're judging the campaign partly by phone calls and a front-desk tally.
- **Prices are public.** Guests compare your rate against every travel site in seconds, so your ad has to show a reason to book direct.

## How should a hotel run Google Ads?

A hotel should run Google Ads in five steps: protect its own name, target nearby searches, connect calls and directions, track real bookings, and set budgets by season. Start with one property and two campaigns before adding anything else.

1. **Brand search campaign.** Bid on your own hotel name so a travel site doesn't get the click.
2. **Local-intent search campaign.** Target searches that include a landmark, an airport, a venue or a neighbourhood.
3. **Business Profile, calls and directions.** Link your Google Business Profile so ads can show a call button and directions.
4. **Conversion tracking.** Count direct bookings, calls and direction requests, not clicks.
5. **Budget and schedule by season.** Raise it before peak weeks and cut it in the quiet ones.

Here's each step in a bit more detail.

### Step 1: Start with your own name

Before I change anything in a hotel account, I search the hotel's name on a phone, the way a guest would, and look at what sits above the hotel's own website. If a travel site is first, that's the first thing I fix. A brand campaign is usually the cheapest traffic in the account because people searching your name already want you.

### Step 2: Target nearby, ready-to-book searches

Use keywords that match how guests describe where they want to stay: "hotel near [airport]", "stay near [conference centre]", "guesthouse in [area]". Keep each ad group tight, and write the ad around the reason that place matters: walking distance, free parking, a quiet street. Add the searches that bring the wrong guests, such as jobs, reviews and cheap hostel searches, to your negative keywords list as you see them in the search terms report.

### Step 3: Connect your Business Profile, calls and directions

Many guests decide on a phone, close to the property. Make sure your Google Business Profile has current photos, hours and phone number, then link it so ads can show a call button and a directions link. A guest who taps "get directions" is a result, even if no booking appears on your website that day.

### Step 4: Track what actually matters

Set up conversion tracking for each of these, and label them separately so you can see what costs what:

- Direct bookings on your booking engine
- Phone calls from the ad
- Direction requests
- Booking engine starts (someone began booking but didn't finish)

If your booking engine can't send bookings back to Google, ask the provider whether it can, and meanwhile keep a simple front-desk tally of "how did you hear about us?". An honest tally beats a dashboard nobody trusts.

### Step 5: Set a budget and a schedule

Work backwards. Hypothetically, say an average direct booking is worth $240 and a travel site would have charged 15% commission on it, which is $36. You can afford to spend well under $36 to win each of those bookings and still come out ahead. Start with the brand campaign and one local campaign, then adjust by season. For how I think about daily spend generally, read [how much to spend on Google Ads per day](/blog/google-ads-cost-per-day).

## Google Hotel Ads and free booking links

Google also has hotel-specific products, including free booking links, that show your rates next to listings on Search and Maps. They usually depend on your booking engine being connected to Google, so the first step is to ask your booking engine provider what it supports. Google explains the setup in its [Hotel Center Help](https://support.google.com/hotelprices). I don't treat this as a replacement for the search campaigns above. It's an extra place to be visible.

## Ad examples you can adapt

These are examples for your own hotel, not real client ads.

- **Brand ad:** "[Hotel name], Official Site. Book direct for free breakfast and the best rate we offer."
- **Local-intent ad:** "5 minutes from [landmark]. Quiet rooms, free parking, easy check-in. See availability."
- **Call-led ad:** "Planning a stay or a group booking? Call the front desk and we'll hold a room for you today."

Replace each offer with something you can really honour. If you advertise free breakfast and the front desk says "not on this rate", you've lost the guest and the trust.

## What I count as a result for a hotel

I judge a hotel account on cost per outcome, not cost per click:

- Direct bookings, and the cost of each
- Phone calls, and how many are real enquiries
- Direction requests
- Booking engine starts

Different outcomes have different value, so I keep them separate. A booking is worth far more than a direction request, but a direction request from someone standing two streets away is worth more than a click from someone browsing.

## Is it cheaper for guests to book directly?

Not always, and guests know that. Travellers often ask "is it cheaper to call a hotel directly?", and the honest answer is that it depends on the hotel's rate rules. What you can control is making the reason to book direct visible in the ad itself: a lower rate, free breakfast, a late checkout, or simply a human to talk to. If direct is no better than a travel site, an ad can't fix that.

## Mistakes I see most

- Not bidding on the hotel's own name, and leaving that click to a travel site
- Competing on broad "hotels in [city]" searches with a small budget
- Measuring clicks instead of bookings, calls and directions
- Running the same budget all year despite a clear peak and off-season
- An out-of-date Google Business Profile, with the wrong phone number or hours
- Sending ad clicks to a booking page that is slow or hard to use on a phone
- Promising a perk in the ad that the front desk doesn't know about

## Frequently asked questions

### How much should a hotel spend on Google Ads?

Work back from the average booking value and the commission you'd pay a travel site for the same guest. Start with a brand campaign and one local-intent campaign, then increase only what produces bookings, calls or direction requests.

### Do Google Ads work for small hotels?

Yes for protecting your own name and for nearby-intent searches. They work less well for broad "hotels in [city]" searches, where large travel sites spend far more than a small hotel can.

### Should a hotel use Performance Max?

It can help with calls and directions when conversion tracking is set up correctly. The hotel on my results page ran Performance Max together with Search. I wouldn't turn it on before the brand and local search campaigns are tracking properly.

### Can I run Google Ads for my hotel myself?

Yes, if you can check the search terms report weekly and your tracking is set up. If you'd rather not, my post on [Google Ads for restaurants](/blog/google-ads-for-restaurants) shows a similar local setup for a different kind of venue.

Bid on your own name, target nearby searches, track bookings, calls and directions separately, and let the season set your budget. Ignore click counts on their own.
