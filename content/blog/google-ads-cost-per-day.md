Google ads cost is the first thing most owners ask me about, and the honest answer is that there is no single price. What you pay depends on your country, your industry and how many competitors want the same search. I'm Aditya, a solo Google Ads and Meta Ads consultant based in India, working remotely with small businesses overseas. Here is how I think about a daily budget.

I've managed 89 ad accounts and ₹89.8L (about $107K) of ad spend, ₹17.6L (about $21K) of it on Google. I can't tell you what a click costs in your city today. I can show you how I decide what to spend, and what happens when the budget is set wrong.

## What does Google Ads cost in the first place?

You pay Google when someone clicks your ad, and each click is priced in an auction. A plumber in a big US city and a café in a small town will pay very different amounts for the same click.

On top of that, if you hire help, there's a management fee. I wrote about how those fees work in my guide on [how to hire a Google Ads consultant](/blog/how-to-hire-a-google-ads-consultant). Your daily budget is the part that actually reaches customers.

## Is $10 a day enough for Google Ads?

It can be, but only for a narrow test. $10 a day is about $300 a month. If you serve one small area and sell one service people search for by name, that can bring in a handful of enquiries. In a market where clicks cost several dollars, it may bring only a few clicks a day.

Here is a hypothetical example, not a quote. If a click cost $2 and one in ten visitors enquired, $10 a day would give you about five clicks and half an enquiry a day. That's about one enquiry every two days. Fine for learning, thin for growth.

## Is $20 a day good for Google Ads?

$20 a day, roughly $600 a month, is a more useful test for most local businesses. You get more clicks, so you learn faster which searches bring enquiries and which waste money.

But "good" depends on what an enquiry is worth to you. If a new customer is worth $500 to your business, paying $40 for an enquiry may be fine. If a customer is worth $50, it won't be.

## What's a good budget for Google Ads?

A good budget is one you can afford to lose while you learn, and big enough to produce a measurable result. I work backwards from three numbers:

1. **What a customer is worth to you.** Be honest about margin.
2. **What you can pay for an enquiry** and still make money.
3. **How many enquiries you need** to tell if it's working, usually a few dozen.

Multiply the second by the third and you have a test budget. Run it for a few weeks, then decide.

## Real numbers from my Google campaigns

These come from Indian accounts, where clicks are far cheaper than in the US, UK, UAE, Australia or Singapore. Don't expect these prices in your market. They show what's possible when the setup is right:

- A restaurant's Google Business Profile ads: 3,857 actions at ₹2.68 (about $0.03) each, on ₹10K of spend.
- A hotel campaign tuned for calls and directions: 1,170 actions at ₹9.87 each, on ₹12K.
- A dry fruit retailer's store-visit ads: 4,050 store actions at ₹25.62 each, on ₹1.04L.

## What a wrong budget costs

The most expensive mistake in my portfolio was a Google call campaign for an education client. I spent ₹4.79L (about $5.7K) for 157 conversions, which is ₹3,043 (about $36) each. I'd used Max Clicks bidding on broad course keywords with no negative keyword list.

The budget wasn't the problem. The setup was. A bigger budget would only have lost money faster. I publish this on my results page because you should see the failures, not just the wins.

Start small, measure cost per enquiry, and scale what works. Don't pick a daily budget by copying someone else's, because their market isn't yours.
