Facebook ads for real estate work when the lead form is built to produce a conversation, and when someone calls the lead back quickly. They fail when the form fills up with names nobody ever phones. I'm Aditya, a solo Meta Ads and Google Ads consultant working remotely from India with owners overseas, and this is how I'd approach facebook ads real estate campaigns for an agent or small agency.

One honest note first: I haven't published a real estate case study on my [results page](/results), so I won't pretend to have one. What I can show is how lead campaigns behave in my other accounts, and the way I'd set this one up.

## Do Facebook ads work for real estate agents?

Yes, they can, because buyers and sellers are on Facebook and Instagram every day and a good listing or valuation offer is easy to show in a feed. But "work" means site visits and valuations booked, not form fills.

Facebook ads for real estate agents usually fail at the follow-up, not the ad. My own results page lists a nightlife venue lead campaign at ₹2,264 per lead (about $27), and the cause was that form leads were never being called back. I rebuilt it around conversations. A lead nobody calls costs you the same as a lead nobody wanted.

## Which lead form setup turns leads into site visits?

Use an instant form with a few real questions, not the shortest form possible. The short form gets the most leads and the worst ones.

Here is what I'd put in it:

1. **A specific offer.** A viewing slot, a free valuation, a floor plan. Not "contact us".
2. **Two or three qualifying questions.** Budget range, buying timeline, and whether they're a buyer or a seller.
3. **Phone number with a confirmation step.** You want to speak to them, so make sure the number is real.
4. **A promise you can keep.** If the ad says "we'll call within the hour", someone has to.

I'd also send each lead to you by WhatsApp or email the moment it arrives, because the first call wins most of the time.

## How much do real estate leads cost on Facebook?

I can't give you a real number for real estate from my own accounts, and I'd be wary of anyone who quotes one without knowing your city and price point. A lead for a rental apartment and a lead for a luxury home are different things.

What I can give you is how lead cost behaves across my accounts. These are from India, where costs are lower than in the US, UK, UAE, Australia or Singapore, so don't expect them in your market:

- A wedding resort: 562 leads at ₹138 each (about $1.65).
- A restaurant party-booking campaign: 301 leads at ₹77 (about $0.92).
- A venue campaign rebuilt as an instant form: ₹45 per lead (about $0.54).

The pattern matters more than the figures: the same business can pay ₹45 or ₹21,691 per lead depending on the objective the campaign is set to. Here is a hypothetical example. If a campaign costs $1,000 and produces 40 leads, that's $25 a lead. If 10 of them take your call and 3 book a viewing, the real cost is about $333 per viewing. That last number is the one to track.
