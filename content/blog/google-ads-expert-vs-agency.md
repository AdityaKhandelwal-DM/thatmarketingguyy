If you're searching for a Google Ads expert, you've probably noticed that most results are agencies, and the "expert" listings are marketplaces. So which one should a small business hire? I'm Aditya, a solo Google Ads and Meta Ads consultant. I'm biased, so I'll try to be fair about when an agency is the better choice.

I've managed 85 ad accounts and ₹86.0L (~$103K) of spend, with ₹17.6L (about $21K) of that on Google. Here is how I'd decide if I were the owner.

## Should I hire an agency or a freelancer for Google Ads?

Hire a freelancer or independent consultant if your ad budget is modest and you want one experienced person who knows your account. Hire an agency if you need several services at once, such as ads, website, design and SEO, and you have the budget to pay for that structure.

The difference is who touches your account. With a freelancer, the person you talk to is the person who builds the campaigns. With an agency, you often meet a senior person in the pitch and then work with a junior account manager.

That isn't always bad. A good agency has process and cover for holidays. But ask one question before signing: who will actually log into my account each week?

For a small business spending a few hundred to a few thousand dollars a month, a specialist usually makes more sense. Agency minimums and overheads are built for larger accounts.

## Google Ads expert or agency: what actually differs?

Here is the practical comparison I'd make:

- **Who does the work:** an expert does it personally; an agency splits it across a team.
- **Cost:** rates vary enormously by market. A US or UK freelancer typically charges several times what an India-based one does for the same work. Most people price either a flat monthly retainer or a percentage of ad spend.
- **Speed of reply:** one person can answer you directly. A team may route you through a ticket.
- **Range of services:** an agency can bundle web design and SEO. A specialist stays focused on Google and Meta ads.
- **Risk:** a freelancer can be ill or busy. An agency can lose your best person and not tell you.

Neither model is automatically better. Match it to your budget and what you need.

## Is Google Ads worth paying for?

Google Ads is worth paying for when people already search for what you sell and you can turn a click into money. A dentist, plumber or clinic gets searched for by people ready to book. That's the strongest case for Google.

It's weaker when nobody searches for your product yet, or when your margins can't absorb the cost of a click. Work out what a customer is worth to you first.

On my [results page](/results) you can see what Google campaigns looked like in India: one physio clinic's YouTube campaign returned 18.6× at ₹4.47 (about $0.05) per conversion, and a hotel got 1,170 actions at ₹9.87 (about $0.12) each. Those clicks cost far less than in the US, UK or UAE, so expect a higher cost per action in your market. The structure carries over, the prices don't.

## What are the downsides of using Google Ads?

The main downsides are cost, speed and control. You pay for clicks whether or not they become customers, and a badly set up campaign can burn money fast.

I've done this myself. On my results page I list a Google call campaign for an education client where ₹4.79L was spent for 157 conversions, because I used Max Clicks bidding on broad keywords with no negative keyword list. I'd rather you hear that from me than discover it from someone else. It's the reason I now look at search terms every week.

Other downsides to know about:

1. **Rising clicks:** popular keywords get expensive when competitors bid.
2. **Learning time:** expect a few weeks before numbers settle.
3. **Tracking:** if calls and form fills aren't tracked, nobody can tell what's working.
4. **Lock-in:** if the agency owns your account, you can lose your history when you leave.

## Who should own my Google Ads account?

You should, whichever route you pick. The Google Ads account must sit under your business, with your expert or agency given access. Never accept a setup where they own it and "lend" it to you. If you part ways, you want your campaigns, conversion data and search history.

## Working with a remote expert across time zones

I'm based in India and work remotely with owners overseas. So far I've run Google Ads for a business in Ontario, Canada and for a film production company in the UK, both through a third party. I'm now taking on direct clients in the US, UK, UAE, Australia and Singapore.

In practice, day-to-day runs over WhatsApp and email, you get a written report on a set schedule, and urgent changes are flagged straight away. The time gap matters less than people expect, because ad accounts don't need a call every day.

## How I'd choose

Ask each candidate three things: who will work on my account, who owns it, and what will the report show? An honest answer to those three will tell you most of what you need.
