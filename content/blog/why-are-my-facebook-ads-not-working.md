Why are my Facebook ads not working? In almost every account I open, it comes down to one of nine problems: the wrong objective, weak offer, tired creative, the wrong audience, impatience, a slow landing page, unanswered leads, or losers nobody switched off. I've managed 89 ad accounts and ₹93.4L (~$112K) of spend across clinics, restaurants, D2C brands, resorts and entertainment venues, and the platform itself is rarely the problem.

Here are the nine, with what each looks like in your Ads Manager and what I actually do about it.

## Why are my Facebook ads not working?

Usually because the campaign is asking Meta for the wrong thing, the offer gives people no reason to act, or nobody follows up on the leads. Check these in order:

1. Wrong campaign objective
2. Optimising for clicks when you sell offline
3. No real offer
4. Creative fatigue
5. Audiences that are too narrow
6. Judging results too early
7. A landing page that loses people
8. Leads nobody answers
9. Losing campaigns nobody cuts

## The 9 reasons, and what I do about each

### 1. Wrong campaign objective

The most expensive mistake I've ever audited: a venue running a **Sales** objective when they wanted leads. Cost per lead: ₹21,691 (~$260). We rebuilt the same audience and creative as an instant-form lead campaign, and cost per lead fell to ₹45 (~$0.54).

Meta optimises for exactly what you ask. Ask for the wrong thing and it delivers the wrong thing, efficiently.

![Reading the numbers in Ads Manager is where every fix starts.](work_charts)

### 2. Optimising for clicks when you sell offline

Traffic campaigns are for profile visits and blog readers. If your business runs on walk-ins, calls or WhatsApp messages, a traffic objective will get you thousands of clicks from people who never buy. Switch to leads or a messaging objective and judge it on cost per conversation, not cost per click.

A rooftop lounge I run did exactly this: instant-form reservation ads replaced generic traffic pushes and brought 382 table leads at ₹38 (~$0.45) each.

### 3. No offer, just "we exist" ads

People don't respond to logos. They respond to a reason to act this week. A D2C skincare serum launch on my results page sat at 0.11× ROAS, and I paused it at ₹4.7K (~$56). The targeting was fine. The offer was wrong, so we rebuilt the funnel before spending another rupee.

### 4. Creative fatigue you haven't noticed

Frequency above 3 means the same people are seeing your ad again and again. Check the Frequency column every month. When it climbs and results fall, the ad is tired, not the platform.

For a farmhouse event venue I run one scaled WhatsApp campaign and refresh the creative the moment it fatigues. It holds at ₹6.36 (~$0.08) per conversation.

### 5. Audiences that are too clever

Stacked interest targeting ("yoga AND organic food AND 30 to 45 AND...") shrinks your audience until Meta can't optimise. My best lead campaign, 2,766 leads at ₹34 (~$0.40) each for a career institute, ran on broad targeting with strong video. The algorithm is better at finding your buyer than your interest stack is. Give it room.

### 6. Judging results in the wrong window

Owners kill campaigns on day two, before the learning phase finishes, then conclude ads don't work. Meta needs a steady flow of results, roughly 50 conversion events a week, to settle. If your budget can't produce that at your current cost per result, optimise for a cheaper event higher up the funnel first.

### 7. The landing page is the problem, not the ad

Here is a hypothetical example, not a client result. Say you pay $0.80 a click, which is fine, but your page takes eight seconds to load on a phone. Most of those people leave before they see your offer, and the campaign gets blamed. Test your own page on a phone, on mobile data, before you blame the ads.

### 8. No one answers the leads

This one hurts the most. A nightlife venue was generating leads at ₹2,264 (~$27) each, which is terrible, but the deeper issue was that nobody called the forms back. We moved to conversation ads so enquiries landed where staff already replied. Leads go cold by the hour. If you can't reply the same day, fix that before you spend more.

### 9. Nobody ever cuts the losers

In my own portfolio I've killed a webinar funnel at 0.07× ROAS and walked a client out of a call campaign that had burned ₹4.79L (~$5,700) at ₹3,043 per conversion. Every account has losers. The difference between profitable and unprofitable accounts is how fast someone notices and stops them. I publish mine, wins and write-offs both, on the [results page](/results).

## Facebook ads not showing at all?

That is a different problem. If your ads aren't spending or aren't delivering, the cause is usually review status, payment, a tiny audience, a cost cap set too low, or a budget too small for the goal. If your ads are spending but bringing nothing back, it's one of the nine above.

Don't judge by whether you can see your own ad in your feed. You may not be in the audience. Use the preview in Ads Manager instead.

## Frequently asked questions

### Why are my Facebook ads getting clicks but no sales?

Usually the objective is clicks or traffic, the landing page loses people on mobile, or the offer gives no reason to buy now. Check them in that order.

### Is it Facebook ads or Meta ads now?

Same thing. Meta is the company behind Facebook, Instagram, Messenger and WhatsApp, and all of their ads are run from Meta Ads Manager. If your Meta ads are not working, the same nine checks apply.

### How long should I wait before deciding Facebook ads aren't working?

Give a new campaign about a week, or until it has left the learning phase, before you judge it. Then judge on cost per lead or sale, not on likes or reach.

### Should I hire someone or fix Facebook ads myself?

If the problem is one of the first four, you can often fix it yourself in an afternoon. If you've already tried and cost per result keeps climbing, a second pair of eyes is cheaper than another month of spend.

## The pattern behind all nine

None of these are "Facebook doesn't work." All nine are settings, creative or follow-up, things you can see in your own Ads Manager once you know where to look.
