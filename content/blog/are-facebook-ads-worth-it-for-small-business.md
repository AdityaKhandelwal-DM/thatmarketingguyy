Are Facebook ads worth it? Sometimes yes, sometimes no, and anyone who says "always" is selling something. I'm Aditya, a solo Meta Ads and Google Ads consultant working remotely from India with small businesses overseas. I've managed 89 ad accounts, so I've seen both outcomes.

Here is how I'd decide, before you spend a dollar or hire anyone.

## Is it worth paying for ads on Facebook?

It's worth it when one customer is worth clearly more than what it costs to win them, and you can track that cost. If either of those is missing, it usually isn't.

Facebook ads (Meta ads, which also run on Instagram) suit businesses that people don't search for every day: clinics, restaurants, venues, D2C brands, local services. You put your offer in front of the right people, and some of them act.

They are a poor fit when:

- You can't say what a new customer is worth to you.
- Your offer is weak or your price is out of line with the market.
- You can't follow up on enquiries quickly. Leads that wait three days go cold.
- You need results next week on a tiny budget.

## Are Facebook ads effective?

They can be very effective, but the platform is only one part. The offer, the creative, the landing page and your follow-up decide most of the result.

Here is what's on my [results page](/results). Across 89 ad accounts I've managed ₹93.4L of ad spend (about $112K), which produced 79,212 leads and conversions. My best ROAS is 8.2× for a textile D2C brand.

A few individual campaigns, each one campaign and not a whole account:

- **Career institute:** 2,766 leads at ₹34 each, on ₹93K of spend.
- **Family entertainment centre:** conversations at ₹18 each, reaching 37.8L people.
- **Skin clinic, Jaipur:** 78% more walk-ins at 5.0× ROAS over 90 days, on ₹8L+ of spend.

I should be straight about one thing. These are Indian campaigns, where clicks and leads cost far less than in the US, UK, UAE, Australia or Singapore. They show the method works. They don't tell you your cost per lead.

## Are Facebook ads worth it for a small business with a small budget?

Yes, if you treat the first month as a test and not a verdict. Small budgets give you less data, so it takes longer to learn what works.

My rule: start with one offer, one audience and a few ads. Judge it on cost per lead or sale. Likes and reach don't pay your bills. If you want the money side in detail, I broke it down in [Facebook ads cost: what I've seen across 89 ad accounts](/blog/facebook-ads-cost).

## Facebook ads for business: how do you know it's working?

You know it's working when you can name three numbers every month: what you spent, how many leads or sales came in, and what each one cost. If you can't, you're guessing.

Work out your break-even first. Here is a hypothetical example, not a real client. If a customer brings you $400 of profit and you can afford to spend up to $100 to win one, then any cost per customer under $100 is worth it. Over $100, it isn't, however pretty the report looks.

Also watch what happens after the click. A cheap lead that never answers the phone is not cheap.

## When should you hire someone instead of doing it yourself?

When your monthly ad spend is big enough that a few percent of waste costs more than the fee, or when you simply don't have the hours. Below that, learn the basics and keep the budget.

If you do hire, ask for three things in writing:

1. The ad account stays in your name, with you as admin.
2. A report on a fixed schedule showing cost per lead or sale.
3. Plain answers about what changed and why.

Facebook ads are worth it for a business with a clear offer, a known customer value and someone who follows up on leads fast. They're not worth it as a hopeful last resort.
