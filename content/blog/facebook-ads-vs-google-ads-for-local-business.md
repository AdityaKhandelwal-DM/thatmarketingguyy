Facebook ads vs Google ads is the question almost every local business owner asks before spending a dollar. I'm Aditya, a solo Meta Ads and Google Ads consultant. I've managed 89 ad accounts and ₹89.8L (~$107K) of spend, and I've seen each platform win and lose for local businesses.

The short version: start where your customers already show intent. Here is how I'd decide, in plain terms.

## Facebook ads or Google ads: which one first?

Start with Google Ads if people already search for what you sell, such as "emergency plumber" or "dentist near me". Start with Facebook ads if people don't search for you yet and you need to put your offer in front of them.

Google catches demand that exists. Someone types a problem, you show up, they call. Meta (Facebook and Instagram) creates demand. It puts your offer in front of people scrolling who weren't looking for you, which suits restaurants, venues, retreats and shops.

Neither is "better" in general. The right first platform depends on whether your customer is searching or needs a nudge.

## Which platform is better for small business ads?

For most local service businesses with urgent needs, Google is the better first platform. For businesses selling something people want but don't search for, Meta is usually better. A small budget works best on one platform, done properly.

Here is the rule of thumb I use:

- **Choose Google first:** clinics, dentists, plumbers, locksmiths, repair services, anything with clear buying intent.
- **Choose Meta first:** restaurants, event venues, resorts, D2C products, gyms, anything people choose on impulse or on looks.
- **Use both:** once the first one pays back and you have budget left over.

Across my accounts, I've run 73 Meta accounts (₹55.3L, 12,550 form leads) and 14 Google accounts (₹17.6L, 60,287 conversions such as calls, direction requests and form fills). I'm comfortable on both, so I'm not pushing you toward the one I like.

## Can the wrong platform cost me money?

Yes, and I've done it. A Google call campaign for an education client spent ₹4.79L (about $5,700) for 157 conversions. I used Max Clicks bidding on broad keywords with no negative keyword list. I also had a nightlife venue's Google Smart campaign get 16,326 clicks and one conversion.

Both are published on my results page under the campaigns I killed. Platform choice matters, but setup matters as much. A good platform with a loose setup still burns money.

## How much should a local business spend to test?

I'd rather not quote a magic number, because it depends on your market and what a customer is worth. What I can say is that Meta needs roughly 50 conversion events a week per ad set before it settles, so on small budgets expect two to four weeks before results mean anything. Google Search can show signal sooner, because intent is already there.

A simple test: pick one platform, one offer and one clear action, such as a call or booking form. Give it a fixed period, track the result, and decide after that.

For a hypothetical example: if a new customer is worth $300 to you and one in five enquiries buys, you can afford to pay up to about $60 per enquiry before breaking even. Work that out before you choose.
