A good Google Ads consultation ends with a plan you can act on, not a sales pitch. I'm Aditya, a solo Google Ads and Meta Ads consultant based in India, and I work remotely with small business owners overseas. I've managed 89 ad accounts, so I've been on both sides of these calls. By the end of this post you'll know what a useful consultation covers and how to spot one that's just a sales call.

## What happens in a Google Ads consultation?

A useful Google Ads consultation moves through five steps in about 30 to 45 minutes. You should leave knowing what to fix first and roughly what it will cost.

1. **Goals and margins.** What a customer is worth to you and what you can afford to pay for one.
2. **Account access.** Read-only access, so the consultant can look without changing anything.
3. **Search terms check.** The actual searches your ads showed for, and what they cost.
4. **Conversion tracking check.** Whether Google is counting real enquiries or junk.
5. **A plan.** A budget and the first 30 days, in writing.

If a call skips steps 3 and 4, it was a pitch.

## What should I bring to a Google Ads consultation?

You don't need to prepare much. These five things make the call far more useful:

- What a new customer is worth to you, even as a rough number
- Your current monthly ad spend (or what you plan to spend)
- Read-only access to your Google Ads account, if you already have one
- Which enquiries actually turned into sales
- Your service area, so the consultant knows where your ads should and shouldn't show

Because I work with overseas owners, I also ask for your time zone and the best way to reach you. Most of the work happens over email and WhatsApp, with a written report on a set schedule.

## What a good consultant checks first

Three things decide whether an account is healthy. A good consultant looks at all of them before talking about budgets.

### Is conversion tracking counting real enquiries?

I check what Google counts as a conversion. If page views or button clicks are counted, the numbers look great while no one is calling. Everything else in the account is steered by that signal, so if it's wrong, the whole account is wrong.

### Which search terms are you paying for?

The search terms report shows what people typed before they clicked. This is where money leaks.

![In a Google Ads consultation, the search terms report shows where the money really went.](work_analytics)

On my [results page](/results) I list a mistake I'd rather you hear from me. An education client's call campaign spent ₹4.79L (about $5.7K) for 157 conversions, which is ₹3,043 (about $36) each. I'd used Max Clicks bidding on broad keywords with no negative keyword list. A proper consultation catches exactly this, and it's why I check search terms early and often.

### Is the account in your name?

The account should belong to you, with the consultant added as a user. If an agency created it under their own manager account and won't hand it over, that's a problem. If you're unsure, ask who the owner is before the call ends.

## How do I find a reliable Google Ads consultant for a small business?

Look for four signals. A reliable consultant shows real numbers including failures, asks about your margins before your budget, gives you ownership of the account, and explains everything in plain words.

For the full checklist, read [how to hire a Google Ads consultant](/blog/how-to-hire-a-google-ads-consultant). If you want to [hire a Google Ads expert](/google-ads-expert) who works on your account personally, that page explains how I work.

A word of caution on proof. My numbers come from Indian campaigns, where clicks cost far less than in the US, UK or Australia. For example, a hotel campaign (Performance Max plus Search, tuned for calls and directions) got 1,170 actions at ₹9.87 (about $0.12) each, with a 47.9% conversion rate. Don't expect those costs abroad. Expect the method to carry over, not the price.

## Red flags on the call

- A guaranteed result or a guaranteed ranking
- "We need admin access, and the account stays ours"
- No questions about your margins or what a customer is worth
- A proposal before anyone has looked at the search terms
- Jargon you can't get a plain answer to

## Frequently asked questions

### Is a Google Ads consultation free?

Many are. Mine is a free first look at your account, and you leave with a short list of what looks wrong or right and what I'd do first. A longer written audit is a separate piece of work.

### How long does a Google Ads consultation take?

A first call usually takes 30 to 45 minutes. A full audit of a live account takes longer, because the search terms and tracking need proper time.

### Do I need to give the consultant access to my account?

Read-only access is enough for a first look, and you should never hand over ownership. Google explains how to add users in its [Google Ads Help guide to account access](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/6372672).

### What is a free Google Ads audit?

It's a review of your tracking, search terms, campaign structure and spend. A good one ends with the three to five fixes that matter most, written down, not a long list of everything that could be better.
